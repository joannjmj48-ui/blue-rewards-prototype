Day one

Build the Goal

Feature 1 · the member says what they want, in their own words

The first question asks what the member is trying to purchase, not what they are saving toward. Members come to a loyalty product to get something and to see what their options are, and saving is one way there rather than the point of it. The rest are the fields a Goal needs anyway, so this is not onboarding. Every answer is optional and the free text is where a sentence like flexible on dates but it needs to be direct becomes a constraint. Me and one other opens the shared Goal, which is a different product, not a setting. The answers resolve into the target underneath: two seats, within two years, 90,000 points, about 26 months at his rate. The caution at the top is lifted from Scenario 4, which states a Goal reserves nothing. Tasks: Create a Reward Goal · Update a Reward Goal.

Two weeks in

What this costs the Goal

Feature 2 · Goal-relative decision preview

Fare comparison already exists on the travel partner's site, so this screen is not a second one. What the partner cannot do is price a fare against this member, because it does not know the Goal, the balance or the date. Those live here. So the comparison here is the Goal against everything the member is weighing, in one unit: months of Lisbon. The hotel night costs seven of them, the grocery card four. Nobody is told which is better and nobody is stopped from taking any of them. The handoff is the open question. The moment the member taps out to book travel, the Goal stays behind, and it should not. Tasks: Compare Redeemable Rewards · Understand the Reward Value · Choose a Redeemable Reward.

The Goal as named stages

Feature 4 · progress framing, with a Goal

The bar answers where am I. The stages answer what is next, which is the question a bar at 27 per cent cannot answer without a dead middle. Both are on screen because members want the summary and the step at the same time, and the stages are named parts of this trip rather than other rewards. New customer · pick what you care about

New feature · the form as a personalization instrument

A new member has no transaction history, so a catalogue ranked by reach has nothing to rank on. Spotify solves the same cold start by asking for genres before it has a single play, and this is that screen for a rewards catalogue: twelve partner and product categories, pick three or more, thirty seconds, no account linking and no permissions asked for. The form is doing two jobs. For the member it makes the first screen worth opening. For the product it is the cheapest, cleanest personalization data there is, because it is declared rather than inferred, and a handful of taps do work that months of behavioural tracking would do worse. The picks are not a filter, so nothing is hidden, and they can be changed any time. That is what makes asking for them legitimate. Compare it with the permission exchange, where the member trades access for a better ranking. Here they trade an answer, and they can take it back.

Outside both journeys

The rest of what the product does. Each one is reached when a member needs it, not as another step on the way to a Goal.
